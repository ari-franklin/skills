# Rewrite Process

## Substance Lock

Before changing style, list mentally:

- Who is the audience?
- What is the ask?
- What claims, numbers, names, dates, and constraints must survive?
- What tone is appropriate for where this will be sent?
- What is the buried punchline?

If the source has ambiguity that could change the ask or facts, ask a
short clarifying question. Do not guess at substance.

## Rewrite Steps

1. Read the source for meaning. Never change the substance, numbers, or
   ask.
2. Dictate the rewrite in your head. If a sentence sounds like a
   document, break it.
3. Choose the context dial from `voice-profile.md`.
4. Add one or two signature moves per passage. Stop there.
5. Keep hedges honest. Hedge only what is genuinely uncertain.
6. Surface the buried punchline and end on it.
7. Do a read-aloud pass.

## Quality Checks

Before returning, verify:

- Every fact, number, commitment, and ask from the source is still
  present or intentionally preserved by implication.
- No new claims were added.
- The rhythm varies naturally and includes fragments only where they
  sound spoken.
- The rewrite uses "so", "and", or "but" instead of corporate
  transitions.
- Signature moves are restrained. One or two is enough.
- Profanity and slang match the audience.
- Ban-list terms are gone unless required.
- The ending lands on the actual point, not a generic summary.

## Common Fixes

- If it sounds too polished, shorten the first sentence and add a
  spoken transition.
- If it sounds too casual, remove profanity, tighten fragments, and keep
  the aphorism.
- If it sounds like parody, remove extra metaphors, repeated catchphrases,
  and fake hesitation.
- If the rewrite got clearer by dropping a fact, put the fact back.
- If the original has no punchline, do not invent one. End on the
  clearest existing implication.
