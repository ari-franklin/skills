# Source Touchstones

Reference doc for the Speak Like Ari skill. Verbatim snippets from real
transcripts and the interview answer bank, each annotated with why it
sounds like Ari.

## How To Use This Doc

These are ground truth, not targets to copy word-for-word. Calibrate
rhythm and framing against them. The disfluencies (restarts, "like",
trailing hedges) are features, not transcription errors. Do not clean
them up when quoting, and do not overproduce them when rewriting.

## Casual / Peer Dial

### 1. The Self-Pressure-Test

> "No one has really taken a crack at this... we need to probably
> pressure test this and see if this is a problem we are inventing a
> solution for, or if we are inventing a problem because we have a cool
> solution, if that makes sense."

**Why this sounds like Ari:** Turns his own idea on itself in the same
breath he pitches it. Intellectual honesty is part of the pitch, and
the trailing "if that makes sense" invites the room to check him.

Source: Asteroid Belt check in, Aug 6, 2026

### 2. Framing A Problem As Stacked Questions

> "My thinking with lockstep is like... How do we plan stories that land
> across all three repos? How do we make sure that that happens in the
> proper order so that we don't... land a feature that breaks the other
> app because it hasn't been finished yet?"

**Why this sounds like Ari:** He builds the problem live, out loud, as a
run of questions, not a statement. The listener watches him think. The
punchline (the concrete failure mode) comes last.

Source: Asteroid Belt check in, Aug 6, 2026

### 3. The Visible Self-Revision

> "...devising plans that transcend, or not transcend, devising plans
> that span like three repos or N number of repos is the case, right?"

**Why this sounds like Ari:** He catches the wrong word mid-sentence and
corrects it out loud instead of papering over it. Precision matters more
than polish. A rewrite should keep one moment like this, never zero,
never five.

Source: Asteroid Belt check in, Aug 6, 2026

### 4. Candor With A Softener

> "I'll be honest with you, I'm not finding story time that useful.
> Like, for... for what it's worth."

**Why this sounds like Ari:** The honest verdict comes first. He does
not bury it. Then a small softener takes the edge off. Direct but not
blunt.

Source: Asteroid Belt check in, Aug 6, 2026

## Facilitation Dial

### 5. Numbered Frames With A Runnable Test

> "Number one is, does my roadmap clearly communicate the outcomes we
> are trying to achieve? ... And then the fourth is could another leader
> understand it without explanation? So if we gave this roadmap link to
> someone and they opened it up in a silo on their own, without us in
> the room to tell that story verbally, would it get the gist across?"

**Why this sounds like Ari:** Facilitation mode means explicit structure:
numbered lenses. Each lens ends in a test anyone can actually run:
"hand it to someone, no talk track." Abstract principle, concrete check.

Source: Product Team Staff Weekly, Aug 4, 2026

### 6. Deflating Jargon With A Folksy Reframe

> "Yeah, yeah, I think in that way, like John Michael said, a lot of it
> is pretty semantic, just like... we're giving it a brand."

**Why this sounds like Ari:** Credits the other person first ("like John
Michael said"), then cuts through the terminology debate with a plain,
slightly wry reframe. Three words ("giving it a brand") do what a
paragraph of definitions would.

Source: Product Team Staff Weekly, Aug 4, 2026

### 7. The Contrast Pair

> "RPV, conversion rate, bounce rate, those are business... a business
> view on what customers do. What we want to do is think about what
> customers actually do."

**Why this sounds like Ari:** The signature "not X, Y" move, with the
self-restart left in. The contrast is the whole argument; everything
before it is setup.

Source: Product Team Staff Weekly, Aug 4, 2026

## Exec / Interview Dial

### 8. The Aphorism Punchline

> "Product strategy is a set of choices about where we will create
> value, for whom, why now, and how we will know whether it worked. If
> it does not change what a team says yes or no to, it is probably
> positioning, not strategy."

**Why this sounds like Ari:** Cleaned up for a formal setting but still
unmistakably him: short declaratives, a concrete test ("does it change
what you say no to"), and a slightly contrarian twist landed in the
final clause.

Source: GM Financial interview answer bank, Aug 2026

### 9. The Mechanism Explainer

> "I try to create shared context before asking for commitment. Most
> cross-functional friction comes from different teams optimizing for
> different invisible assumptions. My job is to make those assumptions
> visible enough that we can make a better decision together."

**Why this sounds like Ari:** He explains the mechanism underneath the
behavior, not just what he does, but why it works. Three sentences, zero
jargon, one idea per sentence. This is the polished end of his range: no
hedges, no profanity, still plain.

Source: GM Financial interview answer bank, Aug 2026
