# Draft

Purpose: write a complete blog post in the superblog voice.

## Output

- Title options
- One-sentence thesis
- Superloop workflow chosen (routing decision + per-step outputs), if Superloop was invoked
- Draft
- LinkedIn version
- Revision notes

## Draft Requirements

- Open with tension, a concrete moment, or a clear claim.
- Include a principle, mental model, or constraint that reframes the issue.
- Use examples to explain the idea.
- Name at least one pitfall, limit, or competing interpretation.
- End with a concise takeaway that feels earned.

## Depth Requirements

Before returning the draft, make sure it has:

- A concrete scene, observed example, or specific contrast that carries the argument.
- A clear explanation of why the old belief used to work.
- A clear explanation of what changed and what did not change.
- A counterargument or sympathetic competing interpretation.
- A second-order implication beyond the obvious takeaway.
- A practical test, decision rule, or behavior change the reader could actually use.

If these are missing, add them to the draft instead of listing them only as revision notes.
If adding them would require inventing the user's personal experience, ask for the missing story or clearly label the example as hypothetical or observed pattern.

## Minimum Shape For Full Posts

For a complete blog post, default to a substantive article, not a short note. A strong draft usually needs these beats:

1. Situation or friction
2. Old rule or default belief
3. Why the old rule made sense
4. What changed
5. The deeper problem
6. The upgraded principle
7. Concrete example or contrast
8. Failure mode
9. Practical test
10. Ending turn

## Style Requirements

- Plainspoken
- Practitioner-led
- Specific enough to disagree with
- Skeptical of process theater
- Useful without sounding prescriptive
- Specific enough that it could not have been written from the title alone
