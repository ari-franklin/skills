# Superblog Core Writing Rubric

Use this rubric with `evals.json`. Judge the requested mode and audience, not an imagined universal article format.

## Scoring

Score each applicable criterion from 0 to 4:

- **4 — Strong:** Fully satisfies the criterion with no meaningful weakness.
- **3 — Good:** Satisfies the criterion with a small weakness that does not impair the result.
- **2 — Mixed:** Partly satisfies the criterion, but the weakness is visible or consequential.
- **1 — Weak:** Shows limited evidence of the desired behavior.
- **0 — Failed:** Violates the criterion or omits the required behavior.

A result passes when all hard gates pass, the average applicable score is at least 3, and no applicable criterion scores below 2.

## Hard Gates

Fail the result regardless of its average score when it:

- Changes or invents meaning, evidence, confidence, names, attribution, numbers, dates, technical terms, links, or required formatting during a rewrite.
- Blurs a fact, interpretation, or recommendation in a way that changes the source's claim.
- Invents personal experience or presents a hypothetical event as something that happened to the user.
- Ignores an explicit output contract or user constraint.
- Uses a prohibited phrase or construction when the prompt explicitly tests its removal.

## Core Criteria

### 1. Abstraction Density

Observable work should remain visible. Flag stacked abstractions such as operating model, strategic alignment, transformation, momentum, maturity, enablement, ecosystem, value creation, and capability building when they replace people, actions, decisions, evidence, or results. A domain term is allowed when it is precise and grounded.

### 2. First-Pass Comprehension

The intended reader should understand each sentence once without rereading. Check for multiple main ideas, long openings before the subject, nested qualifications, unclear pronouns, vague nouns used as verbs, and unexplained internal shorthand.

### 3. Section Uniqueness

Each requested section should do a different job. For weekly updates:

- TL;DR: central movement, tension, and implication
- Completed: verified movement
- Started: active work and blockers
- Next week: observable intended outcomes
- Metrics: current measured state

Fail when the TL;DR merely repeats every later bullet. Apply this criterion only when the format contains purpose-built sections.

### 4. Headline Specificity

The title should name the movement or tension rather than merely announce importance. Prefer clarity over cute or vague headings.

### 5. Evidence-Led Insight

Reasoning should proceed through observation, tension, explanation, and implication. The insight should follow the evidence instead of appearing as an unsupported opening declaration.

### 6. Competing Interpretation

Consequential product lessons, causal claims, trend interpretations, strategic recommendations, and AI predictions should acknowledge the strongest reasonable alternative explanation. It need not receive equal space, but it cannot be silently ignored.

### 7. Manufactured Certainty

Confidence should be proportional to the evidence. Flag certainty markers, universal predictions, and conclusions that generalize from one example. Prefer scoped claims and explicit uncertainty.

### 8. Human Voice

The writing should sound like Ari thinking through real work. Flag generic executive or AI prose, including leverage, unlock value, move the needle, drive alignment, maximize impact, at the end of the day, artificial intrigue, repeated rhetorical triplets, and excessive not-X-but-Y constructions.

### 9. Fidelity

Editing must preserve meaning, evidence, confidence, names and attribution, numbers and dates, technical terms, links and formatting, and the distinction between fact, interpretation, and recommendation. This is a hard gate for rewriting modes.

### 10. Ending Usefulness

The ending should leave the reader with a decision, practical implication, testable question, next action, or concise principle supported by the piece. Fail when it only restates the opening more dramatically.

## Additional Non-Overlapping Superblog Criteria

### 11. Mode Selection

Choose incubate, outline, draft, critique, compress, image-brief, diagram, or substack-html from the user's actual need. Do not produce a polished artifact before the idea or source material can support it.

### 12. Argument Depth

For substantive posts, require a thesis a thoughtful reader could dispute, a concrete example that explains rather than decorates, a credible opposing view, a second-order consequence, and a practical implication that changes a decision. The piece should justify being longer than a social post.

### 13. Grounding and Opening

Open with a concrete moment, friction, or claim that invites the reader into the real pressure. Avoid generic topic introductions and dense abstraction. Label hypothetical or observed scenarios honestly.

### 14. Paragraph Development

Avoid one-sentence body paragraphs, repetitive mini-examples, descriptive sequences that do not advance the reasoning, and sentence rhythms that manufacture punchiness. A paragraph should develop an idea, reason, mechanism, or consequence in direct language.

### 15. Critique Quality

A critique should prioritize the weaknesses that most affect meaning and reader experience, explain why they matter, and give concrete revision moves. It should not merely list preferences or silently replace the draft.

### 16. Compression Without Flattening

Compression should reduce length while preserving the thesis, tension, mechanism, evidence, confidence, and practical consequence. It should not turn the argument into a generic heuristic or slogan.

### 17. Specialized Output Compliance

Image briefs, diagrams, and Substack HTML should be selected only when they reduce reader effort and should follow their mode-specific contracts. Visual output must clarify the argument rather than decorate the topic.
