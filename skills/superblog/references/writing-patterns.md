# Writing Patterns

Use these patterns to shape posts. Do not force every article into the same template.

## Story-Led Principle

Best for: a lesson learned from a real product or leadership moment.

1. Start inside the tension.
2. Show the obvious choices people expected.
3. Introduce the third path or overlooked principle.
4. Explain the mental model.
5. Return to the story and show what changed.
6. Generalize carefully.
7. End with the practical takeaway.

## Anti-Process Reframe

Best for: agile, Lean, XP, product operations, and methodology posts.

1. Name the popular process argument.
2. Show why the argument is understandable but incomplete.
3. Reframe the issue around mindset, craft, values, principles, or constraints.
4. Use a concrete company/team example.
5. Explain what people should do differently.
6. Warn against the new idea becoming dogma.

## Mental Model Explainer

Best for: concepts like real options, constraints, autonomy, resilience, or decision timing.

1. Open with a situation where the reader feels the problem.
2. Define the model in plain language.
3. Show how it changes the decision.
4. Give a few product-work applications.
5. List pitfalls or limits.
6. End with a question or compressed principle.

## Drafting Moves

- Use a concrete problem before abstraction.
- Put the strongest disagreement near the top.
- Include a competing interpretation so the piece does not feel one-sided.
- Let examples carry explanation when possible.
- Make headings clear, not cute.
- End sections with a sentence that advances the argument.

## Revision Moves

- Replace generic claims with a specific workplace pressure.
- Convert advice into a principle plus a constraint.
- Cut throat-clearing introductions.
- Remove any paragraph that repeats the thesis without adding tension, example, or consequence.
- Make the ending more pointed than the opening.
