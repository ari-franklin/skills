# Human-Shaped Language

Human-shaped writing has a lexical fingerprint. It uses specific, sometimes odd words because the writer is reaching for the exact pressure, texture, or failure mode they mean. It does not sound like a median of business writing.

Use this reference when drafting, critiquing, or revising posts where the user cares about voice, originality, or avoiding AI slop.

## Principle

Preserve precision before polish.

The goal is not to sprinkle rare words into the draft. The goal is to keep the words that carry the writer's lived pattern of attention: the metaphors they return to, the verbs they favor, the field language they have earned, and the small strange phrases that make the piece feel authored.

## Keep

- Specific nouns over broad categories: handoff, queue, affordance, constraint, bet, seam, feedback loop, appetite, operating phrase.
- Verbs that show mechanism: compresses, distorts, leaks, exposes, trades, narrows, reframes, compounds, erodes, preserves.
- Phrases that feel native to the writer's thinking when they clarify the claim.
- Slight roughness when it sounds like a person thinking instead of a brand speaking.
- Concrete workplace language: roadmap, backlog, review, incident, sprint, one-pager, dependency, release, support ticket, customer call.
- Odd metaphors only when they explain the idea better than generic phrasing.

## Avoid

- Median business language: important, effective, impactful, scalable, innovative, valuable, seamless, robust, strategic.
- Smoothed-over AI transitions: moreover, additionally, ultimately, in conclusion, it is worth noting, this highlights.
- Abstract filler pairs: clarity and alignment, speed and quality, efficiency and effectiveness, innovation and growth.
- Inflated verbs: leverage, unlock, empower, optimize, drive, enable, transform.
- Forced rarity. Do not use an unusual word just to make the prose look human.
- Sanitizing a memorable phrase because it feels too informal, unless it weakens credibility for the intended audience.

## Revision Test

Before returning a draft, scan for sentences that could appear unchanged in a generic LinkedIn post about any topic. For each one:

1. Replace the broad noun with the actual object, role, artifact, or pressure.
2. Replace the soft verb with the mechanism.
3. Add the missing consequence.
4. Restore any user-provided phrasing that carried useful personality.

Weak:

Teams need to align around outcomes to create better impact.

Better:

Teams need a shared outcome because otherwise every backlog review turns into a proxy fight over whose local urgency gets to win.

## Anti-Slop Gate

A draft is not ready if it is clean but interchangeable. It needs at least three of these:

- A sentence with a specific workplace artifact or moment
- A verb that names the mechanism instead of gesturing at improvement
- A phrase or metaphor that sounds like this writer, not a content template
- A concrete consequence for a real role
- A counterpoint that makes the claim less tidy
- A paragraph that could only belong in this post because of its nouns
