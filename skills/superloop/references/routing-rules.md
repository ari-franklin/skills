# Routing Rules

## Purpose

Determine what reasoning is missing.

Do not select reasoning modes based solely on user wording.

Select the mode that fills the most important reasoning gap.

---

## Understanding Gap

Symptoms:

- ambiguous language
- unclear definitions
- conflicting interpretations
- uncertainty about what is being discussed

Questions:

- What is this?
- What does this mean?
- What are we actually discussing?

Route:

Explain

---

## Decision Gap

Symptoms:

- too many options
- competing priorities
- resource constraints
- sequencing uncertainty

Questions:

- What matters most?
- What should happen first?
- What should we choose?

Route:

Prioritize

---

## Structure Gap

Symptoms:

- complexity
- unclear organization
- undefined components
- execution feels overwhelming

Questions:

- How does this fit together?
- What are the components?
- How should this be organized?

Route:

Decompose

---

## Confidence Gap

Symptoms:

- uncertainty about a claim
- uncertainty about a strategy
- uncertainty about a recommendation

Questions:

- Should we believe this?
- Will this work?
- What assumptions matter?

Route:

Validate

---

## Ambiguous Cases

If the primary reasoning gap is unclear:

- route to Explain
- surface Low Routing Confidence

Do not guess.

Clarify first.

## Direct Routes

Explain is not a required first step. Use a direct route when the object, claim, option set, and decision frame are already clear enough.

Examples:

- Clear option set needing a choice: Prioritize
- Clear claim needing a confidence check: Validate
- Clear large target needing structure: Decompose
- Clear priority call needing risk review: Prioritize -> Validate
