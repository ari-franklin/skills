# Editing Rules

## Signal Before Summary

Do not preserve source order by default. Source order is often conversational, political, or chronological. Smart Brevitas order is reader-first.

First decide:

- What is the news?
- Why does it matter?
- Who needs to act or understand?
- What can be cut?

## Cut List

Cut or compress:

- throat-clearing
- meeting chronology
- repeated acknowledgements
- process narration
- obvious background
- internal acronyms without reader value
- vague optimism
- "we are excited to..."
- "as you know..."
- "moving forward..."
- "circle back," "leverage," "align," "synergy," and similar filler

## Keep List

Keep and sharpen:

- dates
- metrics
- decision requests
- constraints
- owners, if action depends on them
- risks and blockers
- tradeoffs
- user, customer, business, or team impact
- concrete next actions

## Rewrite Rules

- Use active voice.
- Prefer simple words.
- Replace long lead-ins with direct claims.
- Change nominalizations into verbs.
- Use one idea per bullet.
- Bold only the first 2-4 words of important bullets.
- Keep bullets parallel when possible.
- Use numbers when they add clarity.
- Do not over-explain the obvious.

## Headline Rules

Headlines should be:

- under 60 characters when possible
- concrete
- conversational
- news-driven
- useful without the rest of the brief

Avoid topic labels like:

- Weekly Update
- Product Notes
- Executive Summary
- Team Announcement

## Bottom Line Rules

The bottom line should be one sentence.

It should:

- sharpen the takeaway
- make the implication clear
- avoid repeating the headline

Weak:

```markdown
**Bottom line:** The team made progress and will keep working.
```

Stronger:

```markdown
**Bottom line:** The pilot is ready, but Friday's legal review decides whether it ships next week.
```

## Fact Safety

Do not invent:

- numbers
- dates
- owners
- customer quotes
- executive decisions
- source attributions
- public claims

If a needed fact is missing, use:

```markdown
[confirm date]
[confirm owner]
[confirm metric]
```

or state the assumption in the editor's note.
