# Smart Brevity Principles

## Purpose

Use these principles to turn dense source material into fast, useful communication.

## Principles

### Audience First

Before writing, decide who the reader is and what they need from the communication.

Keep only what is new, interesting, urgent, useful, or actionable for that reader. Cut familiar background unless it changes the reader's interpretation.

### Grab Attention

Use a concrete, conversational headline under 60 characters when possible.

The headline should teach the reader something, not label the topic.

Weak:

```text
Project Update
```

Stronger:

```text
Checkout fixes are ready for pilot
```

### Say What Is New And Why It Matters

The lead should answer two questions quickly:

- What happened or changed?
- Why should this reader care?

Put the most important detail first. Do not open with process, background, or agenda.

### Write Like A Human

Use the words a smart colleague would use over coffee.

Prefer:

- short sentences
- active verbs
- concrete nouns
- direct stakes

Avoid:

- corporate jargon
- passive voice
- "leverage," "enablement," "alignment," and similar filler unless source-specific
- inflated importance

### Stay Scannable

Make the page easy to scan:

- short sections
- bullets
- bold scan anchors
- clear labels
- one idea per paragraph

Most paragraphs should be one or two sentences.

### Stop When Enough Is Enough

The goal is short, not shallow.

Cut until removing more would damage meaning, accountability, or actionability.

## Working Standard

Most Smart Brevitas outputs should be materially shorter than the source. Aim for a 30-50% reduction unless the user asks for full fidelity or the source is already concise.
